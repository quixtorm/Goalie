## Part 1. Speech, slide by slide

### Slide 1 · The idea (0:15)
"Hi, we're Team Infinity, and this is Goalie. We help people who get a salary actually reach their savings goals, even though impulse spending keeps getting in the way. Goalie is an AI agent inside the bank's app. It puts aside a safe part of your salary and protects it. Our rule is simple: the code does the math, the AI explains, and you decide."

### Slide 2 · Meet Mubina (0:15)
"Meet Mubina. She's 22, and she's not a real person, she's our example. She earns 7.5 million a month and saves for a car on a separate card. On Friday she goes to dinner and the cinema with friends: 150,000. Feels like nothing, right? But it pushes her car about 3 days further away. And nobody tells her that."

### Slide 3 · Problem and customer (0:25)
"And Mubina isn't alone. We surveyed 16 people. 10 out of 15 who tried to save didn't fully reach their goal. And when we asked why, 9 out of 10 said 'thoughtless spending'. Only 2 blamed emergencies. 11 out of 16 regretted something they bought while saving.
So who's our customer? The user is a person with a salary who's saving for something. But the one who pays is **the bank**. Why? 83% of adults here have a bank card, but only 53% of those cards are actually **active**. And not one of our 16 people keeps savings in a **deposit** or the bank's in-app savings."

### Slide 4 · Solution, live demo (1:10)
*Switch to the app.*
"Okay, let me show you. This is a working prototype.
First, the salary arrives. Goalie splits it on its own: rent goes to a reserve first, then money goes to her goals by priority, and the rest stays on her main card. She didn't have to do anything.
Now Friday. Mubina tries to pay for the cinema with her car money. Goalie stops her: 'Wait, this moves your goal 3 days, and you lose part of your bonus. Want to come back to it tomorrow?' That's the 24-hour pause.
But if it's the pharmacy, it just goes through, no penalty. Health is not an impulse.
Now I just type: 'I want to save 50 million for a car in 3 years.' The AI makes a plan. It uses her worst month of salary, so the plan doesn't break, and shows the best case, the worst case and the end date. And look, nothing happens until she presses 'Create goal'."
*Back to the slides.*
"It can also compare the bank's loans by their real total cost. There's more of that in our video."
*(If the demo breaks, stay on this slide and tell the same story using the four pictures.)*

### Slide 5 · How it works (0:25)
"So, three steps. Goalie understands what you want in normal words. Then the code calculates every number. It's tested code, not the AI guessing. And then you confirm. The AI never moves money by itself.
It's also safe: the **subcards** have no card number, so nobody can pay with them. Anything sensitive needs a second confirmation and a 24-hour wait. In the prototype this part is simulated."

### Slide 6 · Market (0:30)
"Who do we start with? The 11 **mid-sized banks** in Uzbekistan. We think they need something to stand out against the big ones.
Here's our calculation, and to be clear, it's an **assumption** we'll check in a **pilot**: one bank connects 100,000 clients, 10% of them actually use it, and the bank pays 2,000 som per active user per month. That's 240 million som a year per bank. Across 11 banks, that's 2.64 billion, around 220 thousand dollars.
In year one we want 1 or 2 pilot banks, through the hackathon organizers, mentors and the banks' innovation teams."

### Slide 7 · Competitors (0:25)
"What do people use today? Cash, 50%. A separate card, 44%. Nobody in our survey used a deposit or in-app savings. None of these plans from your salary or stops an impulse buy.
Click has an AI assistant called Cleo, which you get with a subscription. From what we saw, it helps you do things: pay, transfer, see reports. Goalie is different: it plans your goals and protects them.
For the bank, the alternatives are building this themselves, their existing savings products, or doing nothing. In a pilot we'll measure how many goals people reach, how many impulse buys we stop, and for the bank, how many users become active."

### Slide 8 · Business model (0:35)
"Who pays? The bank. First a **paid pilot** of 8 to 12 weeks, with the price agreed with the bank. After that, the bank pays per active user per month. Our assumption is 2,000 som. So 10,000 active users means 240 million som a year, about 20 thousand dollars from one bank.
Why this model? Because the bank only pays for people who really use it, and that directly fixes their problem of inactive cards.
How do we get the first 5 to 10 banks? Organizers and mentors introduce us to the first one or two. Then the pilot results help us reach the other mid-sized banks.
Later, maybe, a premium version for users, the way Click already sells its assistant by subscription. But that's an option, not the plan."

### Slide 9 · What we validated (0:25)

A small survey, 16 people, so it's an early signal, not proof. 87.5% would accept locking part of their income until the goal, though some want a 24-hour exit. 81% say the pause with 'your goal moves X days' would help. Everyone would trust an AI to calculate the percent, half of them only if they confirm it. One person wrote: 'Yes, let's do it, just make it reliable.'
Next: a bigger survey, 100+ people, and a pilot with a bank. Our **go signal**: if at least 10% of invited clients become active, the model works and we continue."

## Part 2. Questions the jury might ask

Short, honest answers. If you don't know something, say "we'll measure that in the pilot". Don't make up a number.

### About the problem and the survey

**1. Your survey is only 16 people. Why should we believe it?**
"You shouldn't treat it as proof. It's a first signal from a small, non-random group. That's why our next step is a survey of 100+ people and a pilot with a real bank, and we've set the go signal in advance: at least 10% of invited clients become active."

**2. Most of your respondents are students, but your user has a salary. Isn't that a mismatch?**
"Yes, fair point. 14 of 16 were 18–22, and many are students. The behavior we saw, impulse spending and saving on a separate card, is what we target, but the next survey will focus on people with a salary."

**3. Isn't impulse spending just a personal discipline problem? Why does it need an app?**
"People already try: half save in cash, 44% on a separate card. And still 10 of 15 didn't fully reach their goal. The missing piece is a reminder at the moment of the purchase: 'this costs your goal 3 days'. 81% said that kind of pause would help."

### About the product

**4. What if Mubina really needs the money, say an emergency?**
"Emergencies go through. Pharmacy and hospital purchases are recognized by their **MCC** code and pass with no penalty. For everything else she can still wait 24 hours, pay from her main card, cancel, or spend anyway. Goalie never makes the money unreachable; it makes the cost visible."

**5. What exactly does the AI do, and what does the code do?**
"The AI understands the goal from a normal sentence, explains the plan in simple words, suggests actions, and compares loan options. All numbers (the percent, dates, payments, loan ranking) come from tested code. The AI can only create a proposal card; the person has to press confirm."

**6. What if the AI makes a mistake or invents a number?**
"It can't change the numbers. They come from the calculation code, which has over a hundred automated tests. The AI only puts them into words. And if the AI service is down, the app uses a built-in fallback that gives the same numbers."

**7. Can someone trick the AI into moving money (prompt injection)?**
"No. The AI has no tool that moves money. It can only propose; a person confirms. Whether a purchase is an emergency is decided by code from the MCC code, not by the AI, so a fake shop name can't change it."

**8. How do you pick the percent of salary?**
"Code picks it from the salary only, not from money relatives send. It plans on the worst of the last 6 monthly salaries, so the plan survives a bad month. It finds the smallest percent that reaches the goal in time, and all goals together are capped at 40% of salary."

**9. Why only salary? Many people get money from family.**
"Family transfers are irregular. If the plan depends on them, it breaks. So the plan is built on salary, and when a transfer comes, Goalie just suggests saving part of it, as an option."

**10. What's real and what's mocked in your prototype?**
"Real and working: the prototype, the calculations, the AI agent, over a hundred automated tests, and the video. Mocked: bank data, the loan catalog and rates, and security like the PIN and 24-hour hold. The bonus rates, 22% and 12%, are demo settings."

**11. Does it work in Uzbek?**
"Yes: Uzbek, Russian and English, including the AI's answers."

### About security and data

**12. Is the user's data safe? What does the AI see?**
"The AI gets a short summary: balances, goals and upcoming payments. It doesn't get a name or a card number. Subcards have no card details, so they can't be paid with. In a bank, this would plug into the bank's own login and security; in the prototype it's simulated."

**13. Can a bank legally lock a customer's money?**
"That's exactly the first item in our next-30-days plan: a legal check on locking funds and on the bonus. Also, it's not a hard lock: emergencies pass, and there's always a way out after 24 hours."

### About the business

**14. Why would a bank pay for this?**
"Only 53% of cards in Uzbekistan are active. Banks want people who use the app every month and keep money with them. Goalie gives a reason to come back every payday, and the bank pays only for users who actually use it."

**15. Where does 2,000 som per user come from?**
"It's our assumption, and we say so on the slide. We'll test the price in the pilot. The paid pilot itself is priced together with the bank."

**16. 20 thousand dollars a year per bank is small. Is this a real business?**
"It's one bank with 10,000 active users, so it's a starting point. Revenue grows with active users and the number of banks. And a premium user tier is a possible extra line later."

**17. Why not the big banks first?**
"The 12 largest banks hold about 79% of all assets. They're more likely to build their own. The 11 mid-sized banks need something to stand out, so we think they're a better first segment. That's our hypothesis, and the pilot will test it."

**18. What stops a bank from just building this themselves?**
"Nothing, and that's one of the alternatives we list. Our advantage is speed and a ready, tested product: the calculation engine, the guard and the agent already work. A pilot is cheaper and faster than starting from zero."

**19. How is this different from Click's Cleo or a bank's savings 'piggy bank'?**
"In the screens we checked, Cleo helps you do operations: pay, transfer, see reports. A piggy bank just holds money. Goalie plans the goal from your salary, splits each salary automatically, and stops impulse buys at the moment of purchase."

**20. The credit advisor ranks the bank's own loans. Isn't that a conflict of interest?**
"It ranks by total cost for the customer, not by what's best for the bank. It only shows safe options, where the payment is at most 40% of the worst salary. It shows how each loan delays the goals and compares with saving it yourself. The customer decides, and in the demo the agent never applies for a loan."

**21. How much does the AI cost per user?**
"We haven't measured that yet, so we won't guess; it's on our pilot list. What we can say: the salary split, the purchase guard and all calculations run in code without the AI. The AI is used only when the user chats."

**22. How will you find your first 5–10 banks?**
"The hackathon organizers and mentors can introduce us to the first one or two. A good pilot result then helps us reach the other mid-sized banks through their innovation teams."

**23. How will you measure success in the pilot?**
"For users: goals reached, impulse buys stopped, loans taken only after a load check. For the bank: active users, savings kept in the bank, early signs of missed payments caught. The go signal is at least 10% of invited clients becoming active."

### About the team

**24. Who will keep building this after the hackathon?**
"The five of us. Nodirbek leads product and the prototype, Shohruxbek the presentation, Azizbek checks the surveys and stats, and Suxrob and Abdurahim work on research and next versions. Our next 30 days are already planned."

**25. You said you built it with AI tools. What did you do yourselves?**
"We came up with the idea, did the research and the survey, designed the product rules (salary only, worst-month planning, emergencies pass, nothing moves without confirmation), and checked the results. AI tools helped us build the prototype faster."

## Part 3. Glossary: finance and banking terms

- **Active card / active user:** a card or person that actually uses the service in a period. Many cards exist but sit unused; banks earn from active ones.
- **Assumption:** a number we believe but haven't proven yet. We say it out loud so the jury knows it isn't a fact.
- **Deposit:** money you put in the bank for a fixed time to earn interest.
- **In-app savings feature:** a "piggy bank" inside a banking app (in Russian, «копилка»).
- **Subcard / sub-account:** a separate pocket of money inside your main account, with no card number of its own. In Goalie, each goal is a subcard.
- **Salary only:** Goalie plans only from salary, not from money relatives send, because that money is irregular.
- **Worst case / worst salary:** Goalie plans on your lowest recent monthly salary (from the last 6), so the plan still works in a bad month. The best case uses the average salary.
- **MCC (Merchant Category Code):** a 4-digit code every card payment carries that says what kind of shop it is (cinema 7832, pharmacy 5912). Goalie uses it to tell an impulse from an emergency.
- **Impulse purchase:** an unplanned buy driven by emotion: cinema, restaurants, shopping.
- **Cooling-off period / 24-hour pause:** a delay before an impulse payment goes through, so the urge can pass.
- **Discipline bonus (22% vs 12%):** in the demo, a higher yearly rate on goal money if you don't break the rules. These are demo values, not real offers.
- **Step-up authentication / PIN:** asking for an extra confirmation, like a PIN, before a risky action.
- **24-hour hold:** a sensitive action waits 24 hours and you get a notification, so if it wasn't you, you can cancel it.
- **Prompt injection:** trying to trick an AI with hidden instructions, for example in a shop name.
- **Annual rate:** the yearly interest on a loan, for example 26% a year.
- **Overpayment:** how much more you pay back than you borrowed.
- **Total cost of a loan:** the amount borrowed plus interest plus fees. Goalie ranks loans by this, not by the advertised rate.
- **Origination fee:** a one-time fee for taking a loan. A "0% installment plan" can still have one.
- **Installment plan:** you pay in equal parts over a few months, often marketed as 0%.
- **Safe payment (40% rule):** Goalie calls a loan safe only if its monthly payment is at most 40% of your worst salary, so you can still live and save.
- **Debt load:** how much of your income already goes to debts.
- **Cash gap:** a month where you wouldn't have enough money to cover all payments.
- **Delinquency (early delinquency):** when someone starts missing loan payments. Banks want to spot it early.
- **Savings retained:** money that stays in the bank instead of being spent or withdrawn.
- **Mid-sized bank:** here, a bank with 10–30 trillion som in assets. Uzbekistan has 11 of them.
- **Assets:** everything a bank owns (loans given, cash, securities). It's how bank size is measured.
- **Segment:** the specific group of customers you target first.
- **Pilot / paid pilot:** a limited real test with one bank, a small group of clients and a fixed time. "Paid" means the bank pays for the test.
- **Per active user per month:** the price model. The bank pays for each person who actually uses Goalie that month.
- **Letter of intent (LOI):** a written note from a bank saying it's seriously interested in a pilot. It isn't a contract yet.
- **Go signal / go–no-go:** the result you decide in advance that tells you to continue (go) or stop or change (no-go).
- **Subscription:** a regular payment for a service, like Click selling access to Cleo.
- **Premium tier:** a paid upgraded version for users.
- **Cashback:** part of a purchase amount returned to the customer.
- **Deferral limit:** a "buy now, pay later" limit offered by some apps.
- **Financial inclusion:** how many people have access to and use banking services. The Central Bank's index is where the 83% and 53% come from.
